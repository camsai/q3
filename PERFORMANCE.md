## MPI K-Point Pool Scaling — Cu NC 8×8×8

Parallel scaling benchmark using k-point pool parallelism (each MPI rank owns a pool of
k-points). System: Cu primitive cell with an 8×8×8 k-grid (60 irreducible k-points after
symmetry reduction), norm-conserving pseudopotential, ecutwfc = 120 Ry.

All runs use identical inputs — same geometry, cutoffs, smearing (Gaussian, σ = 0.02 Ry),
XC functional (LibXC PBE), and convergence threshold (10⁻⁸ Ry). Q3 and QE use `--pools N`
and `-npool N` respectively, with pools = np (one rank per pool).

### Scaling Results

| np  | Q3 (s) | Q3 speedup | Q3 eff | QE (s) | QE speedup | QE eff | **Q3/QE** |  ΔE (Ry) |
| --- | -----: | ---------: | -----: | -----: | ---------: | -----: | --------: | -------: |
| 1   |   9.47 |      1.00× |   100% |  16.84 |      1.00× |   100% | **0.56×** | 2.5×10⁻⁷ |
| 2   |   5.35 |      1.77× |    88% |   8.51 |      1.98× |    99% | **0.63×** | 2.4×10⁻⁷ |
| 4   |   3.70 |      2.56× |    64% |   5.55 |      3.03× |    76% | **0.67×** | 2.4×10⁻⁷ |

**Key observations:**

-   **Q3 is ~1.8× faster than QE** at np=1 (Q3/QE ratio = 0.56).
-   **Scaling efficiency is similar** — Q3: 64% at np=4, QE: 76% at np=4.
-   **Energies agree to ~2.5×10⁻⁷ Ry** across all configurations (expected numerical XC noise).
-   **Topology invariance**: Q3 total energy is identical to within 10⁻⁹ Ry across np=1, 2, 4
    (confirming correct inter-pool reductions for charge density, Fermi energy, band energy,
    and smearing entropy).
-   Remaining parallel overhead at np=4 comes from the charge density allreduce on the full
    real-space grid and MPI startup cost relative to the short (~4 s) total runtime. (The
    ~200 scalar inter-pool `MPI_Allreduce` calls the Fermi bisection used to issue per SCF
    iteration were measured here; it now gathers the eigenvalue table once per call.)

### Group 3 — Supercell SCF (3×3×3, Γ-only, displaced geometry)

Starting geometry: thermally distorted 3×3×3 supercell with all atoms displaced ~0.02–0.04 Bohr.
These are single SCF calculations on the displaced structure (with forces enabled), not full
relaxation runs.

| System | PP  | Nat | Q3 E (Ry)     | QE E (Ry)     | \|ΔE\| (mRy) | t_Q3 (s) | t_QE (s) | **t_Q3/t_QE** |
| ------ | --- | --- | ------------- | ------------- | :----------: | :------: | :------: | :-----------: |
| Si     | NC  | 54  | -455.769499   | -455.769493   |   **0.01**   |   39.3   |  125.7   |   **0.31×**   |
| Si     | US  | 54  | -423.491083   | -423.491094   |   **0.01**   |   41.8   |   86.9   |   **0.48×**   |
| Cu     | NC  | 27  | -10438.574366 | -10438.574363 |   **0.00**   |   81.0   |  429.7   |   **0.19×**   |
| Cu     | US  | 27  | -10902.863134 | -10902.862754 |   **0.38**   |   40.8   |   87.9   |   **0.46×**   |

Q3 is **2.1–5.3× faster** than QE on all 4 displaced supercell cases. All energies agree within 0.38 mRy.

**Note**: QE Cu supercell cases use `mixing_beta = 0.2` (matching Q3) since QE's default
`mixing_beta = 0.7` fails to converge for the displaced Cu US 3×3×3 geometry. QE's Broyden
mixer has a hard limit of `maxmix = 25` on `mixing_ndim`, so it uses `mixing_ndim = 8`
(vs Q3's `mixing_ndim = 32`).

**Key observations:**

-   **Q3 is consistently 1.1–2.8× faster** than QE across all 8 primitive and supercell cases
    on a single core. Supercells show larger speedups (up to 2.8×) than primitive cells (1.1–1.5×).
-   **All energies agree within 0.38 mRy** (worst case: Cu US, due to different mixing
    convergence paths). Most cases agree within 0.01 mRy. Primitive cell energies now agree
    to **0.00 mRy** thanks to corrected matching parameters.
-   **Gamma-only force fix** (June 2026): Corrected the nonlocal force and becsum formulas
    for half-sphere G-vector accumulation. Before the fix, spurious forces of ~0.3 Ry/Bohr
    appeared at equilibrium for gamma-only supercell calculations. After the fix, equilibrium
    forces are ~10⁻⁵ Ry/Bohr (correct).

| ------ | --- | --- | ------------- | ------------- | :----------: | :------: | :------: | :------: | :------: | :-----------: |

| Si     | US  | 54  | -423.494426   | -423.494551   |   **0.13**   |    4     |    3     |  245.7   |  388.8   |   **0.63×**   |
| Cu     | NC  | 27  | -10438.575324 | -10438.575297 |   **0.03**   |    4     |    3     |  589.2   |  1085.5  |   **0.54×**   |

Q3 is **1.6–2.4× faster** than QE for all supercell relaxation cases despite sometimes needing
1 more BFGS step, because Q3's per-step SCF is **2.0–3.1× faster**. All energies agree within
0.43 mRy.

**Note on BFGS alpha tuning**: ASE's `BFGS` optimizer was tuned with `alpha=30` (initial
Hessian diagonal) to match QE's step efficiency. This reduces BFGS steps from 4 (default
`alpha=70`) to 3 for Si NC, matching QE's total force evaluation count (4 vs 3). Values
below ~20 cause oscillations; values above ~50 are overly conservative.

## Site-specific measurements

The benchmarks on this page were taken on GitHub Actions runners, which makes them
comparable to each other and reproducible in CI, but not representative of an HPC node.

For NERSC Perlmutter — OpenMP threading, MPI k-point pools and band groups, ScaLAPACK,
and an A100 measured against a Perlmutter CPU node — see
[docs/performance_nersc_perlmutter.md](docs/performance_nersc_perlmutter.md). The short version of
what it found there:

-   k-point pools scale nearly linearly (28.9x on 32 ranks), and are the parallelism
    worth spending ranks on.
-   MPI band groups are slower than serial on a gamma-only system at every width tested.
-   OpenMP peaks at 8 threads and regresses beyond it — 32 threads are 42% slower, and
    64 are slower than 4. Two stages cause it: threaded FFTW is applied _within_ each
    transform rather than across bands, and the dense subspace eigensolve degrades 7.2x
    from 1 thread to 64. The first of those has since been addressed — the band loops in
    the Hamiltonian applicator and the density accumulator now thread across whole
    transforms, each thread driving its own FFT scratch, and FFTW's intra-transform
    threading is switched off whenever that path is available. These numbers predate that
    change and have not been re-measured on Perlmutter.
-   Binding threads (`--cpu-bind=cores`, `OMP_PROC_BIND`) costs about 45% on this
    workload, against the usual advice.
-   An A100 is about at parity with 8 CPU threads on the Cu 3×3×3 system, which is too
    small to occupy the device.

We benchmarked the GPU-accelerated Davidson solver on the GitHub Actions runner equipped with an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU. The system is the large Cu norm-conserving $3\times3\times3$ supercell with 280 bands, 192,228 G-vectors, norm-conserving pseudopotential, and `gamma_only = true`.

All runs use identical parameters: convergence threshold of $10^{-8}$ Ry, 3 SCF iterations, Broyden density mixing ($\beta = 0.20$), and the LibXC PBE functional.

### Computational Environment (GPU)

| Property     | Value                                                   |
| :----------- | :------------------------------------------------------ |
| GPU          | NVIDIA Tesla T4                                         |
| VRAM         | 16 GB GDDR6                                             |
| CUDA Version | 12.8                                                    |
| CPU          | Intel(R) Xeon(R) Platinum 8370C CPU @ 2.80GHz (4 vCPUs) |
| Host Memory  | 16 GB                                                   |

### Benchmark Results (GPU-Resident vs CPU Serial Baseline)

By storing wavefunctions, Ritz vectors, and intermediate solver arrays directly on the device using a unified `GPUDavidsonWorkspace` and executing all linear algebra (including Gamma-only real matrix multiplications via `gpu_blas_.dgemm`) on the GPU, we completely eliminated PCIe page faulting overhead.

This yields a **6.6x overall speedup** and a **10.8x SCF iteration speedup** _against
the CPU serial baseline on the same runner_ — one core of a 4-vCPU Xeon, with no
threading. That is the comparison the table below makes, and it is the right one for
attributing the gain to the GPU-resident solver rather than to the hardware. It is not a
statement of what a GPU is worth against a well-configured CPU node: on Perlmutter the
same system runs in 6.1 s on 8 CPU threads and 6.6 s on an A100, roughly parity. See
[docs/performance_nersc_perlmutter.md](docs/performance_nersc_perlmutter.md).

| Metric / Stage                     | CPU Serial |     CUDA GPU FP64      |  CUDA GPU FP32 Mixed   |
| :--------------------------------- | :--------: | :--------------------: | :--------------------: |
| **Total SCF Time**                 |  411.0 s   |         63.6 s         |       **62.3 s**       |
| **SCF Iteration Time (`iterate`)** |  384.0 s   |         36.7 s         |       **35.3 s**       |
| **Avg. Time per SCF Iteration**    |  128.0 s   |         12.2 s         |       **11.8 s**       |
| **Nonlocal Projector Application** |   83.1 s   | 0.0 s (100% offloaded) | 0.0 s (100% offloaded) |
| **Davidson Solver Loop**           |  335.6 s   |         28.2 s         |       **28.2 s**       |

| — _subspace_update_                |     —      |         1.01 s         |         1.01 s         |

**Key observations:**

-   **Zero PCIe Transfer Overhead:** By keeping wavefunctions resident in the GPU VRAM, host-to-device copying is completely avoided during the Davidson iteration.
-   **Real BLAS Offloading:** Offloading the Gamma-only symmetric projections and subspace updates via real double-precision matrix multiplications (`gpu_blas_.dgemm`) resulted in a **9x speedup** for `orthogonalize` (down from 90.6 s in the regressed version) and an **11x speedup** for `subspace_update` (down from 11.4 s).

The 128-atom cell, where setup is smaller relative to the solver, is the one
whose peak moves: **32% less resident memory for the same answer**. What is left
on the 250-atom cell is the setup spike, filed in [TODOs.md](TODOs.md) §2.3.

Wall time falls on both, most on the larger cell where the pre-reduction binary
is asking a 34 GB machine for more than it has: 522 s against 383 s at 250 atoms,
with 44.3 M page reclaims against 37.2 M.

Projected for `examples/total_energy--semiconductor--silicon/nc_6x6x6.yml`
(432 atoms, 1040 bands, 167,155 plane waves), which fits on neither CI nor a
laptop: 108.6 GB of named arrays per rank before, 52.9 GB after at the expansion
factor of 4 that was the default then, 40.6 GB at the factor of 2 that is the
default now.

Quantum ESPRESSO holds `5 x nbnd x npw` complex for its Davidson and
wavefunctions against Q3's 10 at the default factor and 6 at factor 2, plus the
same projector term in both. Per rank at the same rank count that is 36.4 GB
against Q3's 40.6 GB at factor 2, from that code's own published sizing formula
rather than a run — no such build was available on this machine. The difference that
matters in practice is not the solver: Quantum ESPRESSO divides its plane-wave terms by the
ranks in a pool and Q3 replicates them, so at 16 ranks it needs 2.3 GB per rank
and Q3 still needs 40.6 GB.

That replication is what `--distribute-bands` does; ranks that share a pool
without it now divide reciprocal space by FFT stick and keep the gamma-only half
sphere while doing so. Measured on the 54-atom cell run to convergence with
`--pools 1` (tier-2 reference −455.773125931 Ry):

| Ranks sharing the pool | Total energy (Ry) | Peak resident / rank, max / min |
| ---------------------: | ----------------- | ------------------------------- |
|                      2 | −455.773125930    | 1.60 / 1.53 GiB                 |
|                      4 | −455.773125931    | 0.97 / 0.93 GiB                 |
|                      8 | −455.773125931    | 0.70 / 0.68 GiB                 |

Per-rank memory falls with the rank count, balanced within a few percent, where
`--distribute-bands` holds 3.12 GiB on this deck at every width; repeated runs
spread by about 0.05 GiB. What no longer
shrinks past four ranks is the replicated part: the density G-vector list, the
subspace matrices and the block-diagonal D matrix, filed in
[TODOs.md](TODOs.md) §2.3. The subspace matrices answer to
`method.scf.davidson.basis_expansion_factor`. With the collapse no longer
rebuilding its subspace from the vectors (collapse time 0.53 s to 0.19 s at
factor 4 and 4.1 s to 1.9 s at factor 2 on this run), factor 2 converges the
same four-rank run to the same energy in 93 Davidson iterations instead of 54,
21.4 s of solver time instead of 17.3 s, 32.0 s of wall time instead of 28.0 s,
at an SCF-stage peak of 0.82 / 0.78 GiB instead of 0.93 / 0.90. That trade --
14% of wall time for 12% of SCF-stage memory here, and for 1.0 GB per rank of
replicated subspace on the 432-atom deck -- is why 2 is the default now, as it
is in Quantum ESPRESSO.

The per-rank figures in the two tables above are SCF-stage peaks: until
2026-09-13 the run's own report took its last reading before the forces and
stress. On this deck the forces stage -- a second copy of the projectors and a
copy of the occupied wavefunctions -- used to sit 3 to 7% above the SCF's
plateau at two to eight ranks, because the solver's working set, the projector
cache and the mixing history were still alive under it. The iterator now
releases those once the SCF has converged, and the run's peak is the SCF's;
the report takes a fresh reading at the end and agrees with an external
sampler to 0.01 GiB. The stress, which used to stand far above the SCF at one
rank, has stayed below it since 2026-09-14.

and Q3 (`--pools 1`, stick FFT, gamma-only, the default expansion factor of 2) on the

| ----: | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------: | --------------------: | --------------------------- | ------: |
|     1 | 2.00 GiB                                  |                           0.66 GB |                  74 s | 1.84 GiB                    |    47 s |
|     2 | 0.96 / 0.95 GiB                           |                           0.33 GB |                  44 s | 1.25 / 1.25 GiB             |    33 s |

Quantum ESPRESSO converges to −455.77311913 Ry and Q3 to −455.773125931 Ry, 6.8e-6 Ry apart on 54
atoms: the same 1.3e-7 Ry per atom that separates the two codes on the 2-atom decks
in `reference.json`, so a systematic difference in the functional or projector
integration rather than anything in the parallel layout.

Repeated Q3 runs spread by up to 0.15 GiB at two ranks (1.25 to 1.40 GiB over four
runs) and 0.05 GiB above that; the table carries the last run.

Both one-rank runs include forces and stress. Q3's one-rank row was re-measured on
2026-09-14, after the nonlocal stress stopped building every derivative projector for
every atom at once: it took the run to 4.12 GiB, and working through the cell a few
atoms at a time it stays below the SCF's own peak, so the figure is 1.84 GiB and the
stress itself fell from 3.5 s to 1.9 s. From two ranks up Q3 skips the stress under a
shared pool, and its figure is the SCF's own plateau, 1.3 to 1.5 times Quantum
ESPRESSO's at two and four ranks and 1.9 times at eight.

Where that ratio comes from, read off one rank's address space at the plateau
(`vmmap`, four ranks): 0.32 GiB of heap in use, which is what the run's own
estimate accounts for; 0.12 GiB of code pages; and about 0.35 GiB of heap that was
freed -- the setup transients and the solver's per-iteration temporaries -- and
kept resident by the allocator. Q3 now asks the allocator to return freed pages at
phase boundaries (`release_unused_heap`). Measured, that does not lower the peak
inside an SCF iteration, because the next iteration's temporaries reuse the pages
it would have returned; it lowers what a later phase starts from, which is why the
one-rank figure fell from 4.83 to 4.12 GiB. Lowering the plateau itself means
allocating the per-iteration temporaries once and reusing them, which is filed with
the replicated terms -- the density G-vector list with the Broyden history, and the
subspace matrices held complex rather than real -- as what separates the two
columns.

Quantum ESPRESSO is faster from four ranks on: its all-to-all is on the cutoff sphere and Q3's is
still on the whole box. Q3 is faster at one and two ranks.

## Resolved Performance Investigations

Historical record of diagnosed-and-fixed performance issues. **Open** performance
work is tracked in [TODOs.md](TODOs.md) §2 only (single source of truth) — the
Broyden G-space acceleration and band-parallel-vs-FFTW-threading benchmark items
live there.

-   [x] **Force noise floor for Cu supercells**: Root cause identified as optimizer (QuasiNewton)
        ill-conditioning, not a force formula bug. Q3 analytical forces verified against
        numerical finite-difference (0.06% agreement for Si NC). Resolved by switching to BFGS.
-   [x] **Si supercell relaxation**: Diagnosed. Maxstep=0.2Å causes collective displacement that
        compresses Si–Si bonds, breaking SCF convergence. Fix: default maxstep=0.04Å (already the
        Q3 relax workflow default). Per-step time is 2.0–3.1× faster than QE.
-   [x] **Better optimizer integration**: Switched from ASE `LBFGS` to full `BFGS` with
        `alpha=30` (initial Hessian diagonal). This reduces BFGS steps from 4 to 3 for Si NC
        3×3×3, matching QE's step efficiency. See `src/py/q3/workflows/relax.py`.
-   [x] **Cu supercell relaxation**: Cu NC converges in 4 steps (589s vs QE 1086s, 1.8×
        faster). Cu US converges in 3 steps (146s vs QE 348s, 2.4× faster). All energies
        agree within 0.43 mRy.
-   [x] **FFTW threading for single-rank runs**: When `band_size == 1` (np=1), the MPI execution
        policy now creates a `SerialFFTGrid` with FFTW thread support. Set `OMP_NUM_THREADS=2` for
        ~1.8× FFT speedup on shared-memory architectures. Requires careful `fftw_init_threads` /
        `fftw_mpi_init` ordering (conditional on `OMP_NUM_THREADS > 1`).
-   [x] **Zero-copy FFT in H|ψ⟩**: Added `input_buffer()` / `output_buffer()` /
        `execute_*_on_buffers()` interface to `FFTGrid`. The non-gamma H|ψ⟩ loop now
        scatters/gathers directly to FFT internal buffers, eliminating 4 memcpy per band.
-   [x] **GPU-Resident Davidson Solver & GPU BLAS (July 2026)**: Implemented GPU-resident wavefunction and Ritz vector storage (`GPUDavidsonWorkspace`) to avoid host-device copying. Resolved a severe regression (151.6 s total runtime) where the double-precision Gamma-only (`gamma_only = true`) path fell back to CPU BLAS `dgemm_` on unified device buffers, triggering high PCIe page-faulting overhead. Offloaded the real-symmetric basis projection, candidate Cholesky QR overlaps, and subspace updates to `gpu_blas_.dgemm`, reducing orthogonalization time from **90.6s to 9.96s** (a **9x speedup**) and subspace update time from **11.4s to 1.01s** (an **11x speedup**). This yielded a **6.6x overall speedup** (total runtime of **62.3s** vs CPU **411.0s**) and a **10.8x speedup** on SCF iteration time.
